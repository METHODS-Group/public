About the presentation

  • Topic. A single method that you study beyond the course material: one from the syllabus list (the semi-smooth Newton method, the trust region Newton conjugate gradient method, the Gauss–Newton method, the level set method, the fast marching method, the proximal Galerkin method, the tangential correction method, or the adjoint method for PDE-constrained optimization) or one you propose. The project involves reading a paper or book chapter on the method and implementing it on the class of target problems.
  • The lecture. A one-hour in-class lecture on the paper and your implementation, roughly half the class period. Motivate the problem, derive the method, analyze it (present one or two key proofs), describe your implementation in a finite element library, and show numerical results that verify the theory.
  • Follow the rubric. Prepare your project and lecture according to the course project rubric on Canvas, which sets out what they should include and how they are graded.
